    Pulling in is the dip doing its work, not you failing. The way out is back to front: one small thing before you feel like it. Most people who tried that felt a first lift inside three weeks.

    Flow
    EnergizeDate: 11 / 16 / 26

    Locked in. Time's gone somewhere.

    This is the sweet spot. It lasts about an hour at a stretch and a few hours a day, and a timer protects it better than willpower does.

    An hour at a go; a few hours a day

    JournalCouncil?BeFlow
    How this is built
    Shapearc: Spark and Build on the climb, Locked in at the crest, The tip past it, then two paths down: land (solid, to the baseline) and the crash (dotted, below the line and back up). Both reach the line.
    TruthFlow needs a clear goal and a challenge a notch above skill (Peifer 2022); elite practice sessions averaged 80 minutes and gains stop past about four hours a day (Ericsson 1993); fixed breaks gave less fatigue (Biwer 2023). The 90-minute waking cycle is a rule of thumb, not a clock.
    TimerThe Timer helper counts in the row; it never notifies. No session counts, totals or badges: tracking output cuts enjoyment (Etkin 2016) and rewarding the surge rewards the tip.

    The tip

    Land, or the dip that curves back.

    23:30Flow
    Flow
    EnergizeDate: 11 / 16 / 26

    Wired. One more thing, then one more.

    Wired and scattered means you are past the top, and from here more is not better. Park the ideas, assume everything takes twice as long, and hold the big messages until after sleep. If tomorrow is flat, that is the sleep, not you.

    JournalCouncil?BeFlow
    How this is built
    TruthPast the top, 500 mg of caffeine brought tension and worse performance (Kaplan 1997); people underestimate how long things take (Buehler 1994); sleep loss amplifies reward while raising next-day anxiety about 27% (Gujar 2011; Ben Simon 2020); a five-minute bedtime list sped falling asleep (Scullin 2018).
    CrashNo study measures a post-high crash in people without a mood disorder; the dotted path is built from sleep loss, caffeine withdrawal (peaks at 20 to 51 hours) and cortisol recovery. The page says "usually a day or two".
    BoundaryOne gentle seeded line on the flow at rest: "Highs most days for a week without needing sleep: talk to your GP." Never the word mania.

    Flow
    FightDate: 11 / 18 / 26

    Hot. Leave now. Back at 7:40.

    This is flooding: tunnel sight, tunnel hearing. Nothing said now will land. Twenty minutes is the least it takes; say you are going, and when you will be back.

    JournalCouncil?BeFlow
    How this is built
    Shapemountain: Trigger at the foot, Climb, Flooded at the summit; the summit forks into "say it" (dotted, dips lower through shame) and "leave" (the main slope); both rejoin at Repair on the floor. Faint lines across the slope at 20 minutes and about an hour.
    TruthFlooding sits around 100 bpm with tunnel hearing, and the break is 20 minutes to 24 hours with a return time agreed (Gottman); venting at the peak raises anger, and doing nothing beat venting (Bushman 2002; Kjærvik and Bushman 2024, 154 studies); jogging most raised anger, so the rows are walk, music, shower, breath.
    TimerA return time, not a countdown to calm; the row shows the 20-minute minimum. At the time the page shows a line and asks "slower now?" before "ready?" (a self-rated heat works as well as a pulse, and nobody counts a pulse with tunnel hearing). The "I'm going" line can go as a text to the person it is for (open question 8); nothing else sends, and nothing is scored.

    How this is built
    TruthAdrenaline clears in minutes; cortisol peaks at 21 to 40 minutes and is still falling an hour after (Dickerson and Kemeny 2004); replaying keeps blood pressure up (Glynn 2002); distraction speeds recovery in heavy ruminators (Gerin 2006).

    Flow

    Tomorrow's call. Every version of it.

    The night before is the peak of the curve, not the thing itself. Nine in ten logged worries did not happen. Expect the feeling instead of fighting it; it is already as high as it gets.

    JournalCouncil?BeFlow
    How this is built
    Shapecurve: Something's coming, What if, Night before on the climb; The thing is the vertical drop; It was fine on the flat after, with past outcomes stamped as drawn checks ("didn't happen, 9 in 10").
    TruthEach worry episode kept the heart up about two hours, and relief when nothing happens is what keeps the habit (Newman and Llera 2011); 91% of logged worries in GAD patients did not come true, and marking the outcome is what teaches next time (LaFreniere and Newman 2020); putting worry off to a set time helped in an RCT.

    Replay: the coil

    "Why" tightens it; "how" is the exit.

    11:40Flow
    Flow
    ReplayDate: 11 / 21 / 26

    That look at dinner. Again.

    Every "why" tightens the coil. Swap it for "how did it unfold, moment by moment": that is the swap that loosens it. Eight minutes of something absorbing beats eight minutes of replaying.

    JournalCouncil?BeFlow
    How this is built
    Shapecoil: a spiral in from That stung, Why did I one turn in, Heavier at the centre, and one straight line out of the centre, How not why, with an arrowhead.
    TruthRumination blocks problem solving and predicts the onset of depression (Nolen-Hoeksema 2008); abstract "why" thinking is the harmful mode and concrete "how" the exit (Watkins 2008); eight minutes of distraction beat eight of rumination in the lab. The social hangover after people is this flow (post-event processing, 2024 meta-analysis).
