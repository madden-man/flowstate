## 2. Decisions and assumptions

Decided by Tommy (2026-11-08): the Flow tab is a list of his flows; Panic first, in full; a flow page is its cycle with "where I am" and what to do at that step; fun and interactive, within DESIGN.md; every flow gets its own diagram that tells a truth about that cycle and shows it passes.

Assumed, say so if wrong:

- **The checks and the lean do not vanish, they move.** The cadence checks and the lean slider go under each paradox's spread on Be (a tap on a Be row already opens the spread; the checks and the slider sit under it, Day and Night only). The look back goes entirely: nothing in it was a screen, and the paradox list on Be already shows open balances as dashed slots. `model/flow.ts` keeps its calendar, done-log and lean code; the UI that reads it moves. (Alternative: drop them from the UI and keep the data. One package either way.)
- **Panic ships seeded.** Its four steps, their lines and their things to do are seed data, like the seed voices: Tommy can rewrite, strike, reorder or add to every one of them, and the seed text stays as a first revision. The other flows ship with their steps and tips seeded too, from the research below, once Panic has proven the shape (packages F3 and F4). Nothing is ever generated for him.
- **An episode is a clock, not a score.** Moving the dot writes marks with times, so the page can say "6 minutes in" at the peak and "last time it passed in 48 minutes" afterwards. There are no totals, no "episodes this month", no streaks (rule 15). Still shows no numbers at all: the clock reads in words there.
- **The words on a flow page are stationery, not journal lines.** Rule 16 ("the app never writes a line for you") is about the page. A flow's tips are printed on the page like "No." and "DATE:": the app's print, in Tommy's own phrasing where he has one, editable in the step sheet. Lines in the wiser second person ("You're okay. It's passing.") follow rule 17 and are set in italic print.
- **No reminders, no network, no model.** The flow page is reached by the tab or a deep link; nothing nudges. Recommendations are static seed text from the research, not fetched and not generated.
- **Not a treatment.** The seed tips are the things the clinical guidance agrees on for a person riding out an ordinary episode. The app does not screen, diagnose or escalate, and the plan says so in the one place the research is kept (section 3). If Tommy wants a "call someone" row, it is his line and his number, added in the step sheet.

## 3. The flows: what the evidence says, and what to do at each step

Each flow below records: the cycle in Tommy's words, what the literature confirms or adds, typical durations, the diagram and the truth it shows, and for every step: **what the page says** (the reassurance line, attuned to that step, with its data folded in; this is the step's `said` field, app print, editable), **what to do** (the seed tips, the helper in brackets, the evidence grade), and what to leave out. Grades: **strong** a guideline, meta-analysis or repeated experiments; **moderate** single good studies; **clinical** practice guidance untested as stated; **weak** folk or inference. Sources are numbered in 3.9. Durations are typical, not promises; the UI prints them as "usually".

Not a treatment: the tips are what the guidance agrees on for riding out an ordinary episode. The app does not screen, diagnose or escalate. The one safety line (Depress, the bottom) is the NHS's own.

### 3.1 Panic (the ring)

**Tommy's cycle:** overstimulated, panic state, settle back to discomfort, a gross lingering feeling under the skin that dissipates over time.

**Verdict:** the literature confirms the order and the shape (fast peak, slow fade) and adds a phase before it (anticipation: Clark's loop, a sensation read as danger produces more sensation [P5]; fear of the next attack is what defines panic disorder [P2]) and a strand through the tail (the fear of another one [P3]). No study times all four as a set. Hyperventilation sits inside the build-up: panic patients run low on CO2 and raising it mediated symptom change [P13]. Nothing supports a fixed length for the last step; "panic hangover" of one to three days is a wellness-blog term [P14].

**Durations:** peak within minutes, most attacks 5 to 20 minutes, some up to an hour [P1][P4]. Adrenaline's plasma half-life is about 3.5 minutes, so the pounding fades fast once the alarm stops [P15]; cortisol peaks 21 to 40 minutes after onset and is still falling an hour later, which is why "settling" feels shaky long after the heart has slowed [P11]. Afterwards: "fatigued and worn out" [P3], "tired, take care of yourself" [P16]; hours, no measured length.

**Diagram: the ring.** A drawn circle, four marks, the dot where you are; the arc from Under the skin back to Overstimulated is dotted and carries no step: the way back to ordinary. The truth: it is a loop that closes, and the body's own chemistry is on a clock (the halo on the dot breathes at six a minute at the peak, the rate of the slow-breathing studies [P10]). Phases sit at the four points; the dot rests on the dotted arc between episodes.

|---|---|---|---|

It's draining." ### 3.2 Depress (the valley)

**Tommy's word:** depress. **The cycle the evidence gives** (no source has a validated phase model of a single dip; this is a synthesis of Lewinsohn's reinforcement spiral, behavioural activation and response-styles theory [D6][D7]): Sliding (reward drops first; rumination predicts onset [D7]); Pulling in (less doing, less reward, lower mood, more avoidance: the spiral BA targets [D6]); Head won't stop (abstract "why am I like this" thinking is what makes repetitive thought harmful [D8]); The bottom (empty, no pleasure, tired; two weeks most days is the clinical line [D11]; the daily shape varies: in STAR*D 32% were worse mornings, 49% evenings [D2], so the page never assumes mornings); A little light (first lift is small and early once action starts: 61% halved symptoms by session three in a BA trial [D5]; one 30-minute walk raised vigour within minutes in people with major depression, short-lived [D9]); Climbing out (median episode 3 months in a population cohort; half over by 3 months, 63% by 6, 76% by 12 [D1]; fragile, keep the routine [D11][D12]).

**Diagram: the valley.** A profile: the near slope down (Sliding, Pulling in, Head won't stop), the floor (The bottom), the far slope up (A little light, Climbing out) drawn as long as the near one. On the floor, one dotted arrow up the far slope labelled with the next small thing: the spiral runs outward from one action as surely as inward [D6][D3]. The truth: an episode has a far side (half end inside three months [D1]), and the way up starts with doing before feeling. Percentages are not printed on the drawing (one in five still unwell at two years is not a line for the floor); the shape carries "it ends". The "now" marker is a walker on the slope.

| Step | What the page says | What to do (helper; grade) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Sliding. Usually: hours to days, not measured. | You caught it early, and that counts. A dip starts with doing less, so the routine you keep today is the floor it cannot go under. | Same wake time and meals, whatever the night (sleep-timing variability tracked worse mood as strongly as short sleep, moderate [D10]) · Daylight early (bright light improved remission 41% vs 24%, moderate [D17]) · Put one thing you look forward to on the calendar (scheduling, strong [D6]) · No alcohol tonight; it lifts then drops (clinical [D18]) |
| 2 Pulling in. Usually: days. | Pulling in is the dip doing its work, not you failing. The way out runs backwards: one small thing before you feel like it. People who did that felt the first lift inside three weeks. | The act before the feeling: one small pleasant or mastery thing, scheduled (BA beat controls, SMD .74, strong [D3]) · The smallest next step, easiest first (strong, part of BA [D12]) · Message one person (time with close others cut negative mood more in people with depressive symptoms, moderate [D19]) · Up, not in bed by day (NHS, clinical [D12]) |
| 3 Head won't stop. | The why-questions are the loop, not the answer. Swap one for a how: what happened, and what is one thing you can do now. That swap is the one shown to loosen it. | "How", not "why": what happened, what is one thing I can do (concreteness training cut rumination, moderate [D8b]) · Call yourself by name, or "you" (moderate, not tested in depression [D20]) · Eight minutes of something absorbing (distraction relieves low mood short term, strong [D7]) · Write it down once, then one action (weak) |
| 4 The bottom. Usually: the clinical line is two weeks; most dips are shorter. | This is the floor, and floors are where it turns. Half of all dips are over inside three months, most sooner, and nothing big is asked of you here: water, food, one tiny thing at your better time of day. | One tiny thing, at your better time of day (strong principle, weak timing [D2][D3]) · Eat something, drink water (clinical [D12]) · Music you choose (♪; moderate, therapy not playlists [D21]) · No big decisions now (NIMH, clinical [D11]) · If you are thinking of harming yourself, get help now: NHS 111, or 988 in the US (safety, strong [D12]) |
| 5 A little light. | That lift is real and it came from something you did. A thirty-minute walk lifts vigour within the hour in people with depression; repeat what worked, as small as it was. | A walk, any length (risk falls most at low doses, moderate [D9b]; walking or jogging had a moderate effect in trials [D9c]) · Repeat what lifted you (early BA gains predict outcome, moderate [D5]) · Say what lifted, in plain words, on today's page (Journal; logging raised emotional self-awareness, moderate [D13]) |
| 6 Climbing out. Usually: half of episodes done by three months. | You are on the far slope. Keep the routine that carried you here and add nothing new; climbing is uneven, and a wobble is part of climbing, not sliding. | Keep the routine that worked, no five new goals (weak) · Keep scheduling (BA matched medication for severe depression, strong [D4]) · Expect wobbles (weak) · Go easy: do what you can, decide what can wait (NIMH [D11]) |

**Interaction evidence:** one action, very small (BA grades tasks from easy up [D6]; simple acts become habit faster [D14]); missing a day is harmless (one missed day did not change habit formation [D14]), so nothing ever resets; no streaks or counts (gamification gave no benefit in depression apps [D15]; a broken streak cut later engagement most in people who blamed themselves [D16], and self-blame is a depressive theme); ask "what happened, what did you do", never "why do you feel this" [D8].

### 3.3 Energize (the landing arc)

**Tommy's word:** energize. **The cycle the evidence gives:** Spark (good news, a deadline, coffee, an idea; caffeine's half-life is about 3.4 hours, and a coffee six hours before bed still cuts sleep [E6][E4]; cortisol peaks 21 to 40 minutes after a stressor starts [E7]); Build (moderate arousal helps: 250 mg of caffeine improved performance and mood [E4]); Locked in (flow needs a challenge matched to skill, a clear goal and clear feedback, and sits at moderate arousal, an inverted U [E1][E2]; elite practice sessions averaged 80 minutes and gains stop past about four hours a day [E9]; waking 90-minute cycles are small-study and contested, a rule of thumb not a clock [E13]); The tip (past the top, 500 mg brought tension, restlessness, palpitations and worse performance [E4]; restlessness, irritability and palpitations are the anxiety signature [E14]; people underestimate how long things take, so the high over-promises [E15]; sleep loss makes the tip feel better while making it worse, by amplifying reward [E16]); Landing, or the crash (no study tracks a "post-high crash" in people without a mood disorder, so the crash is built from its parts: one lost night raised next-day anxiety about 27% [E17], sleep loss lowers positive mood [E8], caffeine withdrawal starts at 12 to 24 hours, peaks at 20 to 51 and lasts 2 to 9 days [E5], cortisol is back at baseline within 40 minutes for most stressors [E7]).

**Boundary, not a phase:** hypomania is a distinct state of four or more days with little need for sleep, racing thoughts, fast speech, risky spending [E18]. One gentle line on the page, nothing more: "Highs most days for a week without needing sleep: talk to your GP." The flow is never labelled mania. The 3 a.m. flow's sleep restriction carries the same caution [M7].

**Diagram: the landing arc.** A baseline; the arc climbs from the spark to a crest; from the crest two paths go down: one glides to the baseline ("land"), the other drops below it and curves back up ("crash"). Both reach the line. Phases: Spark and Build on the climb, Locked in at the crest's near side, The tip just past it, Landing or Crash on the two paths. The truth: the energy ends either way, what you do at the crest picks the path, and even the crash curves back [E7][E8]. The hill (inverted U) is the alternative, drawn without numbers because Yerkes-Dodson is a picture, not a law [E3].

|---|---|---|
| 1 Spark | Something lit up, and this is usable. Name the one task before the energy names ten; the sweet spot needs a clear goal more than it needs another coffee. | One task, and its next small step (clear goals come before flow, strong [E1]) · A task a notch above easy (strong [E1]) · Note the last coffee; none within six hours of bed (strong [E6]) |
| 2 Build | Climbing, and the climb is the good part. A notch above easy is where this lands as focus; the second coffee feeds the tip, not the work. | The second coffee feeds the tip (moderate [E4]) · Phone face down, notifications off (a notification alone harms attention, moderate [E19]) |
| 3 Locked in. Usually: an hour to ninety minutes at a time; about four hours a day. | This is the sweet spot. It lasts about an hour at a stretch and a few hours a day, and a timer protects it better than willpower does. | A timer, 25 to 50 minutes, extend once (Timer; fixed breaks gave less fatigue, moderate [E20]; short breaks raise energy d = .35, strong [E10]) · Park new ideas with a when, not into action (plans stopped intrusive thoughts, moderate [E12]) · Cap the block near ninety minutes, the day near four hours (moderate [E9]) |

Park the ideas, double the estimate, and hold the big messages until after sleep. | Check your pulse; racing is the cue to land (strong [E14]) · Out longer than in, under ten a minute (Ring; strong on HRV, moderate on anxiety [E21]) · Write the extra ideas down with a when (moderate [E12]) · Double any time estimate before saying yes (bias strong, fix folk [E15]) · Big messages and decisions after sleep (folk, from [E16]) |
| 5 Landing, or the crash. Usually: flat for a day or two after a push. | The energy ends either way, and what you did at the crest picked the path. Flat and foggy after a push is the sleep and the caffeine, usually a day or two, and not the real you. | Stop while ahead, mid-sentence (folk) · Eat (hunger goes with irritability, moderate [E22]) · Move it into the body: a walk (activity cuts anxiety about -.42, strong [E11]) · Five minutes of tomorrow's list at bedtime (sped falling asleep, moderate [E23]) · Protect the night (sleep loss is the biggest driver of the crash, strong [E8]) · In the crash: low-demand comfort (♪), keep caffeine steady, sleep first, judge later (strong [E5][E17]) |

**Interaction evidence:** a focus session of 25 to 50 minutes with a fixed break, extendable once (fatigue rose faster with rigid Pomodoro breaks [E20][E24]); a "park it" tray during a session [E12]; slow down as landing, not braking ("Good run. Land it while it's good."), never "you're overdoing it"; a one-tap body check (heart racing, hungry, last coffee); a night guard line after a late session ("Tomorrow may feel flat. That is the sleep, not you."); no session counts, totals or badges, since tracking output cuts enjoyment [E25] and rewarding the surge rewards the tip.

### 3.4 Fight (the mountain)

**Tommy's word:** fight. **The cycle the evidence gives:** Trigger (the thing, plus the story about the thing: unexpected, preventable, intentional angers most [F13]; "always" and "never" make it feel justified [F14]); Climb (heat, tight jaw, the sentence forming; irritability appeared in 55% of major depressive episodes [F12], so some of this is the dip talking); Flooded (around 100 bpm, tunnel vision and tunnel hearing: words stop landing [F1][F2]; two exits, blow up or go blank, and going blank is flooding too [F3]); Say it, or leave (venting at the peak raises anger and aggression; doing nothing beat venting, strong [F5][F6]); Cooling (adrenaline clears in minutes [F15]; the 20 minutes is a clinical minimum, not a measured clearance [F1]; cortisol peaks at 21 to 40 minutes and is still falling an hour after [F7]; replaying keeps blood pressure up [F16], so a real cool-down is about an hour, longer if you replay it); Repair (coming back is what builds trust [F1]; what makes a repair land is the friendship, more than the words [F4]; owning it was the strongest part of an apology, asking forgiveness the weakest [F11]; shame "I am bad" feeds more anger, guilt "I did a bad thing" leads to repair [F10]).

**Diagram: the mountain with a fork.** A profile: a steep climb to a sharp summit and a long shallow slope down. The summit forks: "say it" runs longer and dips through shame; "leave" is the shorter road; both rejoin at repair on the valley floor. Two faint lines across the slope: "20 min" (the minimum) and "about an hour" (cortisol). Rumination is drawn as a bump back up the slope. The truth: coming down takes many times longer than going up, every branch reaches the floor, and leaving is the shorter road [F5][F6][F7].

| 3 Flooded | This is flooding: tunnel sight, tunnel hearing. Nothing said now will land. | 5 Cooling. Usually: 20 minutes at least; about an hour; longer if replayed. | The spike cleared in minutes; the slower hormone takes the hour. | 6 Repair | Coming back is the step that builds trust, and the words matter less than the coming back. One short line is enough. | "Can I take that back?", "Let me try again", "I'm sorry" (clinical [F20][F1]) · Own your part in short phrases (moderate [F11]) · Between fights, the friendship is what makes repairs work (clinical [F4]) | |

**Interaction evidence:** a return time, not a countdown to calm: the person picks a clock time 20 minutes to 24 hours away, the page shows the 20-minute minimum, and at 20 minutes asks "pulse under a hundred?" before "ready?" [F1][F2]; a self-rated heat works as well as a reading, precision is not the point [F2]; no sending and no contact shortcuts during the break [F1]; no scorekeeping, ever [F11][F10]; aftermath prompts in second or third person by name [F9]; if the heat shows up most days, a line to the Depress flow [F12].

### 3.5 The states Tommy may have missed

Candidates evaluated: the worry spiral, rumination, shutdown or freeze, the shame spiral, the insomnia night, the social hangover, the avoidance loop, burnout, Sunday or morning dread, doomscrolling, grief. Kept as flows of their own, in the order to build them: Replay, the 3 a.m. night, the What-if, the Shame drop, Shutdown. Folded: social hangover into Replay (post-event processing [M6]); Sunday dread into What-if (a weekly trigger, no separate model); burnout into Energize leading into Depress (little intervention research [M15]); doomscrolling and checking into Avoidance as a safety behaviour [M14]; grief offered as a wave on demand after a loss, not a seeded flow (the dual process model: facing the loss, rebuilding, and breaks from both [M16]). **Avoidance** becomes a step inside Panic and Depress ("do the small version"), not a flow: it is the engine, not a mood [M14].

#### What-if (the curve)

Worry that runs in words, faces the future and builds toward a known event. Each worry episode kept the heart up for about two hours after, and the relief when nothing happens is what keeps the habit [M1]; in GAD patients who logged them, 91% of worries did not come true [M2]. It tips into Panic only at its peak. **Diagram:** a curve that climbs toward the event and drops straight after it, with past logged outcomes stamped on the drop ("didn't happen", nine in ten). The truth: the worry peaks before the thing, not during it, and the record says the thing was fine.

| Step | What the page says | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Something's coming | The dread has a date on it. Worry climbs toward a thing and drops the moment it starts; you are on the climb, which is the only part that hurts. | Write the worry as a prediction, to check later (Journal; weak, one trial of 29 [M2]) · Put the worry off to a set time today (moderate, n = 47 [M3]) |
| What if | Nine in ten logged worries did not happen. That is the base rate for the thing you are picturing. | "What can I do about this, step by step?", not why (moderate [M5]) · The set worry time, then stop (moderate [M3]) |
| Night before | The night before is the peak of the curve, not the thing itself. Expect the feeling instead of fighting it; it is already as high as it gets. | Expect it rather than stop it (weak, preliminary [M1]) · Out longer than in (Ring) · Same wake time tomorrow (3 a.m. flow) |
| The thing | Here it is, and the curve has already turned. | One line: what is actually happening (weak) |
| It was fine | Mark it. The relief should credit reality, not the worrying; this is the line that teaches next time. | Mark the outcome against the prediction (moderate [M1][M2]) |

#### Replay (the coil)

Going back over something already done: a conversation, a look, a failure. Rumination worsens negative thinking, blocks problem solving and predicts the onset of depression [M4]; eight minutes of it in the lab worsens mood against eight minutes of distraction [M5]; after social events it is measured a day to a week later, longer in the socially anxious [M6]. Backward-facing, where What-if faces forward. **Diagram:** a coil that tightens with each "why" and has one straight exit, at "how". The truth: the loop is made of a question, and a different question leaves it [M5].

| Step | What the page says | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| That stung | It landed, and now the replay wants to start. It is a loop with one exit, and the exit is a question. | Name it: this is the replay (clinical) |
| Why did I | Every "why" tightens the coil. Swap it for "how did it unfold, moment by moment": that swap, in the lab, is the one that loosens it. | "How", not "why" (moderate [M5]) · One concrete next step (moderate [M5]) |
| Heavier | Eight minutes of something absorbing beat eight minutes of replaying, in people with depression. Pick the eight minutes. | Eight minutes absorbing (moderate [M4][M5]) · An album (♪) · Call yourself by name (moderate, Panic [P12]) |
| How, not why | You are out of the coil. What happened and what you would do next time, and that is the whole entry. | Write what happened and what next time (Journal; weak) |

#### The 3 a.m. night (the clock)

Awake in bed, checking the time, trying harder. Watching the clock raised worry and lengthened the time to fall asleep in two experiments [M8]; CBT-I works by breaking the link between the bed and being awake, and sleep hygiene alone is not recommended [M7]. **Diagram:** a clock face from 11 p.m. to 7 a.m., the awake stretch drawn as an arc, an "up" arrow out of bed, 7 a.m. pinned. The truth: the morning is fixed whatever the night does, and the bed is for sleeping, not trying.

| Step | What the page says | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Awake again | Awake at 3 is common and it is not a verdict on tomorrow; one bad night changes almost nothing the next day. | Turn the clock away (moderate [M8]) |
| What time is it | Checking the time is the one thing shown to keep you awake longer. The clock is turned away for that reason. | Don't check; the morning is pinned (moderate [M8]) |
| Lying there | Trying harder is the problem. Twenty minutes awake, and the bed is for sleeping: get up, dim light, something dull, back when sleepy. | Up after about twenty minutes, back when sleepy (stimulus control, moderate, guideline [M7]) · Still (dim) · Out longer than in (Ring) |
| Up | Dull and dim. This is not the night failing; it is the bed being retrained. | Something dull in dim light, no screen glare (clinical [M7]) |
| Same alarm | Up at the same time, no nap. That pin is what makes tonight better than last night. | Same wake time, no naps (moderate [M7]) · Daylight early (Depress [D17]) |

Warning carried into Energize: sleep restriction may be unsafe with any history of hypomania [M7].

#### Shutdown (the stairs)

Going still, foggy or far away under overload. The clinical model is the defense cascade: freezing is fight-or-flight on hold, collapse a last resort, and the quiet state afterwards is where rest and repair happen [M9]. Brief unreality is common (26% to 74% of people over a lifetime, about 60% in depression samples, often in panic disorder [M10]), so this may be the tail of Panic. No source times it. **Polyvagal "dorsal vagal" wording is contested** (Grossman 2023; 38 experts signed the verdict in 2026 [M11]); the page uses the cascade's words. **Diagram:** a staircase down (alert, frozen, far away), then a gentle ramp back up labelled "thaw". The truth: the drop is a brake the body pulled, and the quiet after it is the repair, not the damage.

| Step | What the page says | What to do (all clinical practice from [M9], no trials) |
|---|---|---|
| Too much | Your body pulled a brake. It is holding fight-or-flight, not failing; this is a known state with a known way out. | Less coming in (Still) |
| Stuck | Weight first, words later. Feet on the floor, the chair against your back: the body thaws from the ground up. | Feet on the floor; stomp, or shift your weight · Feel the chair pressing your back |
| Far away | Far away is common and it passes; about six in ten people with depression have felt it. A voice or a face brings you back faster than thinking does. | A trusted voice, or eye contact · Slow the breath; over-breathing makes collapse likelier (Ring) |
| Thawing | The quiet now is the repair. Rest is the task. | Rest · An album, or the TV (♪) |

#### Shame drop (the fork)

A sudden collapse after a mistake or being seen. Shame is aimed at the whole self, brings the urge to hide, and pushes blame outward [M12]; it is more tied to depression than guilt is (r = .43 vs .28 [M17]). Its "hit back" branch hands off to Fight. **Diagram:** a drop that forks into "hide" and "hit back", both leading away, and a third path, "I did a thing, I can fix it", curving back up. The truth: the whole-self verdict is the trap, and the one-act version has a road back.

| Step | What the page says | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Seen | Exposed is a feeling, not a fact about you. It peaks fast and the urge it brings (hide, or hit back) is the shame talking, not the next step. | Name it: shame (clinical) |
| I'm worthless | "I am bad" is the whole-self verdict and it is the one that feeds depression. "I did a bad thing" is the version with a door. | Reword "I am bad" as "I did a bad thing" (moderate, correlational [M12]) · Self-compassion (d = .64 on depression, small trials [M13]) |
| Hide or hit back | Both branches lead away from the person and the fix. Notice the urge before you answer; you don't have to take either. | Notice the urge to blame before replying (weak [M12]) · If it is hot: the Fight flow |
| I did a thing | One specific repair, and done. Guilt points at the amends; that is the path that curves back up. | One specific repair (moderate [M12]) · Own it in short phrases (Fight [F11]) |
